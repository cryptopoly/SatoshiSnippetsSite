Not financial advice

SatoshiSnippets is for education and history. Nothing here is financial, investment, tax or legal advice, and nothing here tells you to buy or sell anything. Bitcoin's price can fall a long way quickly, and you can lose money.

Calculators such as the Sats Calculator and Pizza Day use past prices. Past performance does not predict future results. Prices and network figures come from third parties and can be delayed or wrong.

The Ecosystem directory and the Learn page name companies and products so you know they exist. That is not an endorsement. There are no affiliate links or paid placements. Check any service yourself before trusting it with money, and never type your seed phrase into a website.

How we use the Satoshi archive

The texts of Satoshi's 543 forum posts and 39 emails are read from where they were first published: BitcoinTalk, the P2P Foundation forum, and the archives of the Cryptography Mailing List (metzdowd.com), bitcoin-list (SourceForge) and the P2P Research list (a mirror at diyhpl.us). The P2P Foundation forum and the SourceForge message pages are no longer online, so for those we use copies saved by the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine and link the archived copy first, then the original address, which no longer shows the message. The words are Satoshi's.

We do not change Satoshi's words. We do leave some things out: text other people wrote that Satoshi quoted in a reply (for forum posts the name of the person he replied to is kept), the “so-and-so wrote:” line above such a quote in an email, and the footer the mailing-list software added to every message (such as unsubscribe instructions). The texts are shown as plain text, so forum formatting, pictures and smiley icons are not shown, and the fixed line breaks inside email paragraphs are joined up. Search Satoshi otherwise shows each message in full; the other pages quote short excerpts. Dates are shown in UTC.

From the Satoshi Nakamoto Institute we use three things: its list of which posts and emails are Satoshi's (we checked the BitcoinTalk part against BitcoinTalk's own list of posts by the user satoshi, and the two match); its selection of 162 Satoshi quotes and the topics it files them under, which Satoshi Says shows alongside our own picks (each quote's wording is checked against the original post or email); and the titles that 10 BitcoinTalk threads had before their starters renamed them. The Institute's website says its content is licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution-ShareAlike 4.0 licence, and that some works may be subject to other licences. We read this from the data files in the Institute's public code repository, whose software is under the GNU AGPL v3; we use none of that software.

Where that licence applies to what we use, it asks us to credit the Institute, link the licence, say what we changed, and share what we build from that material under the same licence. So: the selection of quotes on Satoshi Says and the list of items in Search Satoshi, as published here, are shared under CC BY-SA 4.0, and you may reuse them on the same terms. What we changed: the quotes are filed under our own topics, each quote shows the date of the post or email it comes from, and quotes from the whitepaper have the paper's reference numbers put back.

Privacy

There are no accounts or sign-ups on this site, nothing you type into a calculator is sent anywhere, and we do not collect your name, email or anything else that identifies you. This site sets no cookies, which is why there is no cookie banner.

We run our own copy of Umami (open-source analytics) for visitor statistics. It does not use cookies. It counts page views and records the referring site, browser, operating system, device type and approximate location (country), which we only see as totals.

Some pages load live data straight from mempool.space and CoinGecko, and map and globe graphics from the jsDelivr CDN. Like any website, those services see your IP address when your browser contacts them; their own privacy policies apply.

A few tools remember things in your browser's local storage, such as your Call the Block score. That data stays on your device; clearing your browser's site data removes it.
